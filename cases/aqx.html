Context

Stone's design team needed a fast, consistent way to evaluate the quality of a product experience without relying on one reviewer's subjective read. AQX, our Experience Quality Assistant, is the agent we built to do that: it's trained on our own quality framework and evaluates a micro-journey (one task, roughly 5–7 screens) against 17 criteria across 4 pillars, returning an A–E verdict.

It rates each criterion based solely on visible evidence: it never infers what isn't on screen. More than a score, AQX gives the team a shared vocabulary: instead of loose impressions, we name specific criteria backed by evidence, decide what should block a release, and turn every gap into a prioritized backlog opportunity.

What it doesn't replace: research with real users, usability testing, or business metrics. AQX is a fast, heuristic analysis layer built on criteria the team defined itself. The more context a designer provides, the more precise the read.

The four pillars

The 17 criteria split across four pillars, each answering a different question about the experience:

  • Intuitive (8 criteria). Resolves: completes the task without making the user stop to think.
  • Humanized (4 criteria). Recognizes: treats whoever's on the other side of the screen as a person.
  • Proactive (3 criteria). Anticipates: guides the next step instead of leaving the user to figure it out alone.
  • Memorable (2 criteria). Differentiates: makes the experience unmistakably Stone.

The 17 criteria

Split into two blocks. The 10 functional criteria are non-negotiable: they're the gate. The 7 differentiation criteria elevate the experience above that baseline.

Functional · 10: the foundation that has to work

  • I1 Is simple
  • I2 Prioritizes what matters
  • I3 Helps you remember
  • I4 Has exits
  • I5 Is consistent
  • I6 Prevents and handles errors
  • I7 System feedback

Differentiation · 7: what makes the experience unmistakable

  • H3 Empathy in critical moments
  • H4 Inclusion and representation
  • P1 Is predictive
  • P2 Is decisive
  • P3 Is personalized
  • M1 Our brand is present
  • M2 Microinteractions that celebrate

How it works

AQX is an orchestrator: it doesn't analyze everything itself, it coordinates a set of specialized skills, each responsible for one stage:

1

Captures the context

A quick intake interview (anamnesis) gathers scope, platform, screen type, and scenario: improving, evaluating something new, or comparing versions.

Scoring model

The 10 functional criteria are non-negotiable: they're the gate. The 7 differentiation criteria elevate the experience above that baseline. There's no weighted 0–100 score, since that used to mask functional failures. When every functional criterion is adherent, the letter comes from how many of the 17 are adherent overall:

  • A: Exceptional (17/17)
  • B: Highly adherent (14–16/17)
  • C: Adherent (12–13/17)
  • D: Adherent with debts (10–11/17)
  • E: Non-adherent, a functional criterion that was read fails to meet the bar, regardless of the count.

If a functional criterion simply couldn't be read from the material provided, AQX doesn't fail the journey for lack of input. Instead, it returns Incomplete analysis along with exactly what to send to finish. Non-adherent is always a signpost for the team to act on, never a grade someone failed.

Output

Here's what that looks like against a real journey: Instant Pix with an available balance, evaluated end to end, and the prioritized opportunity list AQX returned alongside its verdict.

High

Completes the analysis and unblocks the functional gate

  1. Design the Pix error states (invalid key, insufficient balance, wrong password, failure), the biggest unlock for the verdict.
  2. Replace the deprecated component on the contacts screen with the current Jade component.
  3. Escalate the primary button's contrast to the Design System (3.16:1 < 4.5:1), a token debt that affects every experience using the button.
Medium

Differentiation

  1. Celebrate completion (M2): a microinteraction on the receipt turns the confirmation into a memorable moment.
  2. H3/H4 move out of N/A once the errors get empathetic copy and the flow has inclusive illustration.
Low

Observed refinement

  1. Voice (H1): "Who's this Pix for?" or "How much do you want to send?" instead of "Enter the key" or "the amount."
  2. Prototype data: recipient and amount vary between screens, aligning them makes the review easier to read.
